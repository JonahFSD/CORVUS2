# Two-minute competition pitch v0.1

- **Audience:** General pitch-competition judges
- **Status:** Demo-aligned working script
- **Target spoken length:** 250–270 words
- **Date:** 2026-08-25

## Spoken script

### 0:00–0:22 — Hook

> Your behavior is already being modeled. The objective just is not your wellbeing.
>
> The world’s largest technology companies combine streams of behavioral data to predict the next click, purchase, or conversion. They do not need to understand your mind perfectly. They only need to predict you well enough to change what you do.

### 0:22–0:40 — Inversion

> Wayfinder inverts that system. It is a personal model you own, built from signals you choose to share, and optimized for outcomes in your real life—not engagement with an app.
>
> We are starting with one concrete problem: helping someone who has moved to a new city turn new acquaintances into real connection without burning themselves out.

### 0:40–1:22 — Demonstration

> Here, the participant’s recovery is far below their personal baseline. They also describe a fun group dinner, one promising new connection, and feeling too depleted for another event.

**Select _Update my model_.**

> The language model turns their words into inspectable evidence. It does not decide how they feel. A separate probabilistic model weighs that evidence against their personal history, updates six narrow dimensions, and shows its uncertainty.
>
> The result is not a diagnosis. It is a correctable hypothesis: their desire for connection is still present, but their capacity is temporarily down. Every inference can be traced back to its source and corrected by the participant.

### 1:22–1:47 — Useful action

**Point to the recommendation.**

> A generic chatbot might say, “Go socialize more.” Wayfinder says: protect tonight, then preserve the warmest relationship thread with one low-pressure coffee invitation tomorrow.
>
> The person remains in control. Wayfinder never sends the message. If they try the suggestion, what happens next becomes new evidence.

### 1:47–2:00 — Vision

> This is the beginning of participant-owned state estimation: the engineering pattern behind behavioral targeting, redirected toward human agency, stronger relationships, and less dependence on AI over time.
>
> The future should not be AI that knows how to use you. It should be AI that helps you know what works for you.

## Presenter runbook

1. Load `/` and leave the page in its initial state.
2. Deliver the hook while the title and two consent promises are visible.
3. Point to the wearable and journal cards during the concrete scenario.
4. Select **Update my model** at approximately 0:52.
5. Point first to receptivity and recovery load, then to the interpretation card.
6. Optionally open **Inspect the evidence** if judges care about trust or technical differentiation.
7. Point to the dark recommendation panel and select **I’ll try this** before the final line.
8. Use **Reset the pitch demo** before the next presentation.

## Claim discipline

- Say **probabilistic personal model**, not validated machine-learning system.
- Say **wellbeing pattern tracker**, not psychiatrist, therapist, diagnosis, or treatment.
- Say **synthetic wearable snapshot**, not a live WHOOP account.
- Say **correctable hypothesis**, not a fact about the participant.
- Say **personal experiment**, not N-of-1 trial.
