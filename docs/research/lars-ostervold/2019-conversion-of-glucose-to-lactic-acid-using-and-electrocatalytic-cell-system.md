## Summary

The abstract motivates an alternative route for converting glucose to lactic acid. It contrasts fermentation, whose downstream separation can be costly, with inorganic catalytic approaches intended to favor lactic acid over competing products. It also notes that thermocatalytic systems had obtained substantial yields but often required elevated temperatures or otherwise demanding conditions.

The proposed electrochemical approach targets two steps discussed in the thermocatalysis literature: glucose–fructose isomerization and retro-aldol cleavage of fructose to three-carbon intermediates. The working hypothesis was that an applied potential could shift the fructose/glyceraldehyde portion of the network toward the cleavage product and thereby accelerate a limiting step. Copper oxide was selected because divalent-metal catalysts had performed well in prior thermocatalytic systems.

## Planned experiments

- Measure lactic-acid yield across a range of applied potentials.
- Begin with copper oxide as the working electrode.
- Follow with copper oxide deposited on a conductive carbon electrode.
- Seek useful yield under milder conditions than the thermocatalytic comparators cited in the abstract.

## What this record does and does not establish

The abstract establishes the early research question and proposed experimental direction. It does not establish that voltage changes the proposed equilibrium, that copper oxide improves selectivity, or that the system outperforms fermentation or thermal catalysis. Those propositions required prospective measurement.
