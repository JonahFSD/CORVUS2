## Scope and chapter progression

The dissertation uses glucose-to-lactic-acid conversion as a test case for rational electrocatalyst design. It moves from proof of concept, to identification of the copper state present under reaction conditions, to cation-assisted yield improvement and catalyst restructuring, and finally to enabling instrumentation for operando X-ray absorption measurements.

### Chapter 2: proof of concept

The first study applies mechanistic ideas from thermocatalysis to a polycrystalline copper electrode. It reports a maximum lactic-acid yield of 23.3 ± 1.2% under the explored electrochemical conditions. The dissertation treats this as proof of concept but notes that the yield did not match thermocatalytic studies reporting more than 75%. This result overlaps the 2021 journal article and is not an independent replication.

### Chapter 3: copper state under reaction conditions

Operando Raman spectroscopy is paired with density-functional-theory Raman calculations. The dissertation assigns the active copper form to CuOOH, probably as hydroxide adsorbed on a CuO surface, written CuO–OH*. That is a spectroscopy-and-model-supported structural assignment, not a direct image of the active site.

### Chapter 4: barium-assisted yield increase

Calculations favored fructose adsorption through its O1 and O3 atoms. Because cleavage of the C3–C4 bond is central to the proposed retro-aldol step, the work hypothesized that a divalent cation interacting with exposed O2 and O4 atoms might weaken that bond and change activity or selectivity. Adding Ba²⁺ increased the reported lactic-acid yield by 277%, to 64.6 ± 2.8%. The dissertation describes this as the highest yield then reported for an open-to-atmosphere, room-temperature process using an earth-abundant catalyst. Both the mechanism and priority claim should be read with the author's qualifications and comparator set.

### Chapter 5: catalyst restructuring in barium hydroxide

X-ray absorption measurements compare copper in potassium hydroxide and barium hydroxide and support formation of BaCuO₂. The dissertation concludes that Ba²⁺ substantially changes catalyst structure. It explicitly leaves unresolved how the resulting BaₓCuᵧO_z material interacts with glucose, so the structural observation and the proposed molecular interaction with fructose should not be collapsed into one demonstrated mechanism.

### Chapter 6: operando XAS cell

The final research chapter presents a versatile electrochemical cell for operando X-ray absorption spectroscopy, with provisions for liquid flow, gas flow, pH and temperature monitoring, and multiple catalyst formats. Its stated aim is to reduce the engineering overhead for future operando studies.
