## Evidence status

**Observed measurements and simulations as reported by the authors; structural assignment is an interpretation.** The paper reports operando Raman measurements, electrochemistry, and density-functional-theory calculations. The assignment of the relevant state to CuOOH and its likely surface geometry is model-mediated rather than a direct image of an atomic structure.

## Research question

Copper oxide can be active during the alkaline oxygen-evolution reaction, but the composition and electronic character of the highly oxidized copper state often labeled “Cu³⁺” were unresolved. The study asks which local atomic environment is consistent with the Raman feature that appears under strongly oxidizing conditions.

## Approach

The authors combined:

- operando Raman spectroscopy under electrochemical control;
- density-functional-theory electronic-structure calculations;
- calculated Raman spectra used as theoretical standards for candidate copper oxyhydroxide structures; and
- electrochemical constraints used to eliminate candidates that were inconsistent with observed behavior.

## Reported findings

The Raman experiments showed a distinct feature associated with highly oxidizing conditions. Multiple computed Cu³⁺-containing candidates could reproduce aspects of that signal, so the spectral match alone was not treated as uniquely identifying the phase. When the calculated candidates were combined with electrochemical evidence, the authors assigned the active stoichiometry to CuOOH and proposed that it most likely exists as hydroxide adsorbed on a CuO surface.

Electronic-structure analyses described in the author-maintained abstract indicate that oxidation removes electron density substantially from oxygen 2p states. The authors therefore argue that a ligand-hole description, often written as d⁹L with Cu²⁺ character, is more faithful than interpreting “Cu³⁺” as a purely local formal oxidation state on copper.

## Interpretation and limits

- “Cu³⁺” is a formal label in this work, not a claim that all missing electron density is localized on a copper atom.
- The CuOOH conclusion is an assignment supported by agreement among spectroscopy, calculation, and electrochemical constraints; it remains conditional on the candidate structures and models evaluated.
- The paper says the hydroxide is *likely* surface adsorbed. The summary preserves that uncertainty.
- Activity under oxygen-evolution conditions and co-occurrence of a spectral state do not, by themselves, prove that one uniquely identified site causes the measured catalytic rate.
