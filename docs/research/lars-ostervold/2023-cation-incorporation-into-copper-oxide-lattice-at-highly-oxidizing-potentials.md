## Evidence status

**Observed spectra and post-reaction characterization as reported by the authors; phase assignments are model-supported interpretations.** The work combines operando X-ray absorption spectroscopy with electronic-structure modeling, near-edge simulation, and post-reaction characterization.

## Research question

Electrolyte cations are known to affect electrocatalytic kinetics and selectivity. The paper tests a less typical mechanism: whether an electrolyte cation can enter the bulk lattice of an oxide electrode during electrochemical oxidation, rather than acting only through interfacial fields, solvation, adsorption, or double-layer effects.

## Approach

Copper electrodes were followed during alkaline electrochemistry in potassium hydroxide and barium hydroxide electrolytes. The comparison uses operando X-ray absorption near-edge and extended-fine-structure measurements. Structural hypotheses were evaluated with electronic-structure calculations, simulated near-edge spectra, and characterization after reaction.

## Reported findings

The spectra recorded in potassium hydroxide were consistent with prior copper-oxide observations, whereas those recorded in barium hydroxide differed substantially. The authors propose the following potential-dependent sequence, with potentials reported versus the normal hydrogen electrode:

1. Above approximately 200 mV, Ba²⁺ enters the copper-oxide lattice and an ordered BaCuO₂ phase forms.
2. At 900 mV, further oxidation produces a bulk, Cu³⁺-like BaₓCuᵧO_z phase.
3. During subsequent reduction in barium hydroxide, Cu–O bonding and Cu–Ba scattering persist down to −400 mV.

The paper presents this as evidence for direct oxidative incorporation of an electrolyte cation into a bulk mixed-oxide electrode. The authors suggest that such incorporation might offer a route to forming oxidation electrocatalysts in situ.

## Interpretation and limits

- The phase sequence is proposed from convergence among spectroscopy, simulation, and post-reaction evidence. It is not a direct atom-by-atom observation.
- “Cu³⁺-like” is deliberately retained because formal oxidation-state language does not uniquely specify where charge is localized.
- The authors' “first evidence” statement is a priority claim qualified by “to our knowledge,” not an independently exhaustive proof about all earlier literature.
- Formation of a mixed oxide is observed under the reported conditions; whether it improves activity or selectivity for a particular reaction requires separate comparative catalytic measurements.
