## Evidence status

## Purpose and design

The article presents a two-compartment flow cell for operando X-ray absorption spectroscopy in electrocatalysis. Its purpose is to reduce repeated, laboratory-specific cell-design effort while retaining flexibility across experiments. The design supports optional electrolyte flow, gas flow, pH monitoring, temperature monitoring, and catalysts placed on electrically conductive substrates that are sufficiently transparent to X-rays.

The paper includes engineering drawings, computer-aided-design files, and a parts list. Positioning the catalyst at the X-ray window reduces the path through attenuating material and permits measurements on thin films or deposited catalyst layers.

## Evaluation

The authors benchmarked spectra from the new flow cell against an existing single-chamber acrylonitrile-butadiene-styrene batch cell. In their stated comparison, the flow-cell spectra had comparable quality and were collected with 92-second scans rather than 300-second scans. The comparison is useful but not perfectly controlled: the samples and geometries were not identical, and the benchmark contrasts a thin film with nanoparticles.

Operational checks reported in the manuscript include:

- no bubble interference across roughly −1000 to +1000 mV versus the normal hydrogen electrode under the tested flow conditions;
- leakage testing in which failure began near 25 mL/min per compartment at the plugs; and
- routine experiments at 10 mL/min per compartment.

Two reference-electrode arrangements are discussed. A Luggin-capillary configuration was more stable and was used for a gas-flow test lasting over 20 hours, but it was more complex and could trap bubbles in tubing. A compact reference was easier to use but more susceptible to drift and bubbles.

## Demonstration study

The cell was used to compare copper thin films in barium hydroxide and 200 mM tetrabutylammonium hydroxide. Copper oxidized substantially in the barium-containing electrolyte. In tetrabutylammonium hydroxide, Cu–Cu scattering remained evident through 900 mV and Cu–O scattering was small. The authors interpret this as tetrabutylammonium preventing bulk copper oxidation and suggest that site blocking may be involved.

## Interpretation and limits

- The cell's gas-flow, pH, and temperature options were described, but not all were exercised in the article's experiments.
- Required catalyst loading depends on the material, edge, and beamline; the paper cannot supply one universal optimum.
- The benchmark demonstrates feasibility, not superiority under every sample and geometry.
- The tetrabutylammonium experiment establishes a strong electrolyte-dependent structural difference under the tested conditions. It does not resolve the molecular mechanism of inhibition, and the proposed site-blocking explanation remains a hypothesis.
